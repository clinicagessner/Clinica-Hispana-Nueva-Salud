Diabetes is one of the most common conditions in the Hispanic community: about 1 in 8 Latino adults in the United States has it, and many more are living with prediabetes without knowing it. The good news is that, with proper management, you can live a full, active life. This guide explains the essentials in plain words, so you know what to watch, what to eat and when to see a doctor.

## Know your numbers

The first step is knowing where you stand. These are the values your doctor checks and what they mean:

- **Fasting glucose:** your blood sugar when you wake up. Normal: under 100 mg/dL. Prediabetes: 100-125. Diabetes: 126 or higher on two readings.
- **Hemoglobin A1c:** your average blood sugar over the past 3 months. Normal: under 5.7%. Prediabetes: 5.7-6.4%. Diabetes: 6.5% or higher. If you already have diabetes, the goal for most people is **under 7%**.
- **Blood pressure:** the general goal is under 130/80.
- **Cholesterol and triglycerides:** they often go hand in hand with diabetes and multiply the risk of a heart attack.

Write down your results every time you get [blood tests](/en/services/examenes-sangre). Seeing how they change over time is the best way to know whether the plan is working.

## Signs your sugar is high or low

**High sugar (hyperglycemia):** intense thirst, frequent urination, blurry vision, tiredness, wounds that are slow to heal. If it's constant, your treatment needs adjusting.

**Low sugar (hypoglycemia):** shakiness, cold sweat, dizziness, intense hunger, confusion. It can happen if you skip meals or take more medication than needed. Have something sugary right away (juice, 3-4 candies) and check your glucose after 15 minutes.

If you have low-sugar symptoms often, tell your doctor: it's a sign the dose needs review.

## Food is your medicine

It's not about giving up what you love, but about balance. Tips that actually work in a Hispanic family kitchen:

1. **Cut back on sugary drinks:** sodas, juices and sweetened aguas frescas are the easiest sugar source to eliminate. Switch to water, water with lime or unsweetened tea.
2. **Watch portions of tortillas, rice, bread and potatoes:** no need to remove them, but limit them to one serving (2 tortillas, half a cup of rice) and pair them with protein and vegetables.
3. **Fill half your plate with vegetables:** nopales, squash, chayote, green beans, salad. They're cheap, filling and barely raise your sugar.
4. **Choose beans from the pot** over refried beans with lard, and **grilled or stewed meats** over breaded or fried.
5. **Don't skip meals.** Eating 3 times a day at regular hours prevents spikes and crashes.
6. **Whole fruit, not juice:** one orange, yes; a glass of orange juice equals 3-4 oranges without the fiber.

## Move every day

A 30-minute daily walk helps lower your sugar, blood pressure and weight, and makes you feel better. You don't need a gym: walk around your neighborhood, dance, climb stairs, play with your kids. If it's been a while since you exercised, start with 10 minutes and build up. If you use insulin, ask your doctor how to adjust to avoid lows.

## Check your feet and your eyes

Diabetes can damage nerves and circulation without you noticing. Check your feet every day for blisters, cracks, redness or sores; if you find anything, get [wound care](/en/services/curacion-heridas) as soon as possible. Get a dilated eye exam once a year, even if you see well.

## Follow-up makes the difference

Poorly controlled diabetes can damage the kidneys, eyes, heart and nerves, but that damage takes years and can be prevented. That's why [regular follow-up with your doctor](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) is key: generally, A1c every 3 to 6 months, full [blood tests](/en/services/examenes-sangre) once a year (kidney, cholesterol, [thyroid](/en/services/tiroides)) and, if your doctor recommends it, an [electrocardiogram](/en/services/electrocardiograma). At each visit the treatment is adjusted to your numbers, not the other way around.

## What if I was told I have prediabetes?

It's your best chance to avoid diabetes. Losing 5 to 7% of your weight (about 10-15 pounds if you weigh 200) and walking 30 minutes a day cuts the risk of progressing to diabetes by more than half. A yearly glucose and A1c check tells you whether you're on track.
