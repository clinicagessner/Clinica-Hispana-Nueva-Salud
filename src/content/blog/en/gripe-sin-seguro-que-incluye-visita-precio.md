You wake up with a fever, body aches and a scratchy throat. If you don't have health insurance, the first question on your mind is often not "what do I have?" but "how much is it going to cost to get checked?". That worry keeps a lot of people at home treating themselves, and many end up coming in only after several rough days.

This guide doesn't go back over the general options for people without coverage (our article on [healthcare without insurance in Houston](/en/blog/atencion-medica-sin-seguro-houston) handles that). It focuses on this season: which parts a flu visit at the clinic can include, which ones depend on your symptoms, and how to ask for the price up front without feeling awkward about it.

## A flu visit, broken into its parts

A visit for respiratory symptoms isn't a fixed bundle. It's put together based on what's going on with you. These are the parts our [flu, COVID and respiratory illness service](/en/services/enfermedades-respiratorias) describes:

| Part of the visit | When is it done? | How does it help you? |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom evaluation and a check of throat, ears and lungs | At every visit | Tells whether it's mild or something to watch more closely |
| Rapid influenza (flu) test | When symptoms point to the flu | Confirms or rules out the flu, with a result in minutes |
| COVID-19 test | When symptoms overlap | Tells you whether to take extra care around family |
| Strep test | If the sore throat suggests it | Shows whether an antibiotic is needed or not |
| Treatment and prescription | When the medical team starts treatment | You leave with a clear recovery plan |
| Home-care instructions, in Spanish | At every visit | You know what to do over the next few days and when to return |
| Doctor's note | If you ask for one for work or school | Proof that you were seen |

Seeing it this way helps because you can ask what each part costs on its own. A visit where you're only examined is not the same as one with two tests.

## Not every test is needed every time

Having three rapid tests available doesn't mean you'll get all three. The clinic's medical team decides based on what they find when they examine you:

- **A sudden high fever, body aches and heavy tiredness** suggest the flu, and the rapid test helps confirm it.
- **Fever, cough and tiredness**, sometimes with loss of smell or taste, can be COVID-19; confirming it helps you protect the people at home.
- **A sore throat that comes on fast, red tonsils or white patches, and swollen glands in the neck** point toward strep.
- **Coughing, a stuffy nose and hoarseness** point more toward a virus, and then a strep test usually isn't needed.

If you're told a test isn't necessary, nobody is cutting corners on you: it just wouldn't change the treatment. And if you want to know why a test is being recommended, ask. You have every right to know what each thing you pay for is for.

One detail about the [strep test](/en/services/prueba-strep) is worth knowing: if it's negative, antibiotics won't help, because they don't work against viruses. Treatment then focuses on easing symptoms with fluids, pain relief and rest.

## Before you call: what to have written down

A call or text goes much further when you already know what to say. Keep these details handy:

1. **When your symptoms started.** If it turns out to be the flu, the start date matters for treatment, since some medicines work best in the first two days of symptoms.
2. **What bothers you most:** fever, sore throat, cough, body aches, congestion.
3. **Whether you have a condition** like asthma, diabetes or high blood pressure, are pregnant or are over 65. In those cases the flu deserves an earlier check.
4. **How many people at home are sick.** If two or three of you are coming, ask the price per person so the total doesn't catch you off guard.
5. **Whether you need a note** for work or school, so you can mention it from the start.

## Getting a price quote over the phone or on WhatsApp

At our clinic you pay directly, and we tell you the price before any test or procedure. Even so, asking before you leave home lets you show up knowing what to bring and without the stress. You can call or send a WhatsApp message; the buttons are on every page of the site.

A message like this one is enough. Copy it and adjust it:

> Hi, I've had a fever and a sore throat since yesterday and I don't have health insurance. How much is the visit? Are the flu, COVID and strep tests charged separately? How much is each one? Two of us would be coming in.

Questions worth asking every time:

- **What does the visit price include?** Just the exam, or a test too?
- **Does each rapid test have its own price?** That way you can make an informed choice if the medical team recommends one.
- **Are there any current promotions?** Whatever is running is posted on our [promotions](/en/promociones) page.
- **How can I pay?** The clinic takes cash and debit or credit cards: Visa, Mastercard, American Express and Discover.

If you're already in the exam room and something you hadn't planned on is suggested, ask the price right then. It's a normal question, and nobody will mind.

## The flu shot: for later, not for today

While you're running a fever, your job is to get better. Once you feel well again, the [annual flu vaccine](/en/services/vacunas) lowers your risk of getting seriously ill for the rest of the season. It goes in the arm and is quick. If someone at home hasn't had theirs yet, especially older adults, pregnant women or people with diabetes, asthma or high blood pressure, you can ask about its price on the same call.

If you live with a chronic condition, the flu can throw your blood sugar or blood pressure off. Our [chronic condition management](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) service helps with follow-up once the infection has passed.

## When not to stop and ask about prices

Some situations aren't the time to call around. Skip the price call and head to the ER, or dial **911**, when anyone in the household shows:

- Trouble breathing or shortness of breath
- Pain or pressure in the chest
- Lips or face turning blue or purple
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness or being hard to wake up
- Seizures
- A fever or cough that gets better and then comes back worse

In those cases, care comes first. Everything else can be sorted out afterward.
